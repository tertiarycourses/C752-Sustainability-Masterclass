## Topic 1: Analyse Carbon Emission Hotspots

Standards, boundaries, data, scopes and hotspot diagnosis

Learning outcome: LO1

### A Carbon Inventory Is a Decision System

Purpose: Translate physical activity into comparable tCO₂e for decisions.

Boundary: State which entities, operations, gases and period are included.

Method: Use consistent activity data, emission factors and global-warming potentials.

Evidence: Retain sources, assumptions, controls and recalculation rules.

### Build a Corporate Inventory in Five Moves

1. Set organisational boundary

2. Set operational boundary

3. Collect activity data

4. Calculate and consolidate

5. Report, verify and improve

### Five Principles Protect Decision Quality

Relevance: Reflect the organisation and users’ needs.

Completeness: Account for all sources within the boundary; disclose exclusions.

Consistency: Enable meaningful comparison over time.

Transparency: Disclose methods, assumptions and evidence.

Accuracy: Reduce bias and uncertainty as far as practical.

### Organisational Boundary Choices

| Equity share | Control approach |
|---|---|
| Account according to economic interest | Account for operations under financial or operational control |
| Useful where ownership shares matter | Often aligns with management systems |
| Apply consistently across investments | Document the chosen control test |

### How the Standards Fit Together

1. ISO 14001 structures the EMS

2. GHG Protocol structures corporate accounting

3. ISO 14064-1 supports organisation-level quantification

4. ISO 14064-3 supports verification

5. Assurance strengthens confidence

### Accounting Standard vs Management Standard

| GHG Protocol / ISO 14064 | ISO 14001 EMS |
|---|---|
| What and how much is emitted | How environmental performance is managed |
| Boundaries, categories, calculations | Policy, objectives, controls and review |
| Inventory quality and reporting | Continual improvement through PDCA |

### The Seven Greenhouse Gases

CO₂: Fuel and process emissions

CH₄: Waste, agriculture and fugitive emissions

N₂O: Combustion, fertiliser and processes

HFCs: Refrigeration and cooling

PFCs: Electronics and industrial processes

SF₆: Electrical equipment

NF₃: Electronics and semiconductor manufacturing

### From Activity to tCO₂e

A transparent inventory keeps every assumption visible.

Emissions (tCO₂e) = Activity data × Emission factor × GWP ÷ unit conversion

- Example: kWh × kg CO₂e/kWh ÷ 1,000
- Use the factor for the correct geography and year
- Record factor source, unit and uncertainty

### A Practical Data-Quality Ladder

1 · Supplier-specific: Verified primary data for the exact product or service.

2 · Metered activity: Invoices, meters, fuel records or logistics data.

3 · Engineering estimate: Measured drivers with documented assumptions.

4 · Spend proxy: Currency × environmentally extended input-output factor.

5 · Unsupported guess: Use only temporarily; flag for improvement.

### Three Scopes Prevent Category Confusion

Scope 1: Direct emissions from sources owned or controlled.

Scope 2: Indirect emissions from purchased electricity, steam, heat or cooling.

Scope 3: Other value-chain emissions upstream and downstream.

Rule: Scopes describe responsibility for accounting—not moral blame.

### Location-Based and Market-Based Views

| Location-based | Market-based |
|---|---|
| Average grid emissions where consumption occurs | Contractual instruments and supplier attributes |
| Useful for physical system context | Requires quality criteria and evidence |
| Singapore example: EMA grid factor | Report both where applicable |

### Follow the Value Chain, Not the Org Chart

Upstream: Purchased goods, capital goods, fuels, transport, waste, travel, commuting and leased assets.

Downstream: Distribution, processing, use, end-of-life, leased assets, franchises and investments.

Screen first: Estimate all relevant categories before investing in precision.

Engage next: Improve supplier and customer data at material hotspots.

### A Hotspot Is More Than the Largest Bar

Magnitude: Absolute or intensity-based share of emissions.

Influence: Ability to change technology, demand or supplier behaviour.

Risk: Carbon price, regulation, disruption or reputation.

Strategic fit: Connection to customers, growth and operational priorities.

### Common Inventory Failure Modes

Missing sources: Completeness check against sites, ledgers and processes.

Double counting: Clear ownership, category definitions and consolidation rules.

Unit errors: Automated validation and unit conversion controls.

Wrong factor: Versioned factor library with geography and year.

Silent estimates: Estimate register with owner and replacement date.

### Reading Singapore Electricity Data Correctly

Published factor: EMA reported 0.402 kg CO₂/kWh for 2024.

Correct year: Match the factor year to the reporting period.

Correct unit: Convert kg to tonnes only once.

Record evidence: Save the factor source and access date.

Recalculate: Define when base-year electricity emissions are restated.

### Separate Carbon Value From Cash Value

Carbon effect: Annual and lifetime tCO₂e avoided.

Cash effect: Capex, opex, savings and residual value.

Risk effect: Carbon tax, energy volatility and compliance exposure.

Strategic effect: Resilience, customer value and learning.

### Simple Payback Is Fast—but Incomplete

Useful as a screening metric when cash flows are stable.

Simple payback (years) = Initial investment ÷ Annual net cash saving

- Ignores time value of money
- Ignores cash flows after payback
- Never rank long-lived projects by payback alone

### NPV Makes Timing Explicit

Discount future net cash flows to present value.

NPV = Σ [Net cash flowₜ ÷ (1+r)ᵗ] − Initial investment

- Positive NPV creates financial value at the chosen discount rate
- Test energy price, utilisation and maintenance assumptions
- Use sensitivity analysis instead of false precision

### Compare Projects per Tonne Avoided

Marginal abatement cost exposes the price of each carbon outcome.

MAC = Lifetime net cost ÷ Lifetime tCO₂e avoided

- Negative cost means the measure saves money
- Use consistent lifetime and discounting
- Do not ignore feasibility and dependencies

### Illustrative Abatement Portfolio

| Category | Illustrative value |
|---|---|
| LED | -55 |
| Controls | -20 |
| Solar | 18 |
| EV fleet | 65 |
| Green material | 110 |

Negative-cost measures can help fund strategic measures with positive abatement cost.

### Stress-Test the Variables That Matter

Energy price: Low, expected and high tariff cases.

Utilisation: Operating hours, throughput and occupancy.

Performance: Degradation, rebound and implementation shortfall.

Carbon value: Tax, internal carbon price or customer premium.

Discount rate: Financing and risk assumptions.

### Two Ways to Price Carbon Internally

| Shadow price | Internal fee |
|---|---|
| Apply a hypothetical carbon value in investment cases | Charge emissions and recycle funds into reductions |
| Changes decisions without charging business units | Creates a visible operating signal |
| Good for stress-testing long-lived assets | Needs governance and acceptance |

### LED Retrofit Decision

Illustrative teaching data: S$120,000 capex; S$38,000 annual saving; 72 tCO₂e avoided per year; 8-year life.

Payback = 120,000 ÷ 38,000 = 3.16 years
Simple lifetime MAC = (120,000 − 8×38,000) ÷ (8×72) = −S$319/tCO₂e

- The project saves money and carbon
- Confirm maintenance, hours and baseline
- Use discounted cash flow for approval

### From Standards to a Defensible Hotspot

Frame: Purpose, organisational boundary, operational boundary and period.

Quantify: Activity data × factor × GWP with controlled units.

Diagnose: Rank magnitude, trend, risk and controllability.

Value: Combine tonnes, cash, risk and strategic fit.

### Evidence Should Survive the Handover

1. Source register

2. Calculation workbook

3. Quality checks

4. Hotspot narrative

5. Investment assumptions

## Topic 2: Prioritise Reduction Initiatives

Projection, technology, economics and transparent decisions

Learning outcome: LO2

### A Baseline Is a Managed Reference Point

Base year: Representative, reliable and relevant to the target.

Structural change: Acquisition, divestment or outsourcing may require recalculation.

Method change: Material factor or methodology changes may require restatement.

Growth: Separate absolute emissions from intensity and activity growth.

### Start With the Business-as-Usual Case

Make growth, efficiency and factor assumptions explicit.

Future emissions = Future activity × Future emission factor × (1 − efficiency improvement)

- Build the no-action case first
- Avoid claiming reductions caused by grid decarbonisation as operational savings
- Use compatible units and periods

### Absolute and Intensity Targets Answer Different Questions

| Absolute target | Intensity target |
|---|---|
| Total tCO₂e falls against baseline | tCO₂e per unit of output or revenue |
| Directly aligned to atmospheric outcome | Separates growth from efficiency |
| Can challenge a fast-growing business | Can improve while total emissions rise |

### Build Scenarios as Decision Stories

1. Define drivers

2. Choose coherent assumptions

3. Model business-as-usual

4. Layer reduction measures

5. Compare emissions, cost and risk

### Use Ranges Where the Future Is Uncertain

Low case: Slower growth, lower adoption or conservative performance.

Expected case: Best current estimate with documented evidence.

High case: Faster growth, price shock or implementation upside.

Trigger: Define the observation that causes a plan revision.

### Illustrative 2030 Emissions Path

| 2027 | 91 |
| 2028 | 83 |

### Explain Why Emissions Changed

Separate operational performance from structural and market effects.

Δ Emissions = Activity effect + Efficiency effect + Factor effect + Structural effect

- Activity effect captures volume or growth
- Efficiency effect captures operational improvement
- Factor effect captures energy or supplier decarbonisation

### Avoid the Rebound Trap

Efficiency gain: Less energy per service unit.

Behaviour response: Lower cost can increase demand or operating hours.

Net result: Gross saving minus additional consumption.

Control: Track both intensity and absolute activity.

### Electrifying a Singapore Delivery Fleet

Illustrative: 20 vans × 35,000 km/year; diesel van 0.25 kg CO₂e/km; EV 0.20 kWh/km; grid factor 0.402 kg CO₂/kWh.

Diesel = 175 tCO₂e/year
EV electricity = 56.3 tCO₂e/year
Screening reduction = 118.7 tCO₂e/year

- Add vehicle and battery lifecycle where material
- Model future grid factors separately
- Validate actual route, charging and payload data

### Reduce Before You Compensate

Avoid: Remove unnecessary demand, travel, material or process.

Reduce: Improve efficiency and yield.

Substitute: Use lower-carbon energy, material or transport.

Engage: Work with suppliers, customers and employees.

Neutralise residuals: Use credible removals only after deep reductions.

### Energy Levers: Measure, Control, Replace

Measure: Sub-meter material loads and normalise for production.

Control: Schedules, set-points, maintenance and automation.

Upgrade: Efficient motors, chillers, compressed air and heat recovery.

Replace: Renewable electricity and electrified heat where feasible.

### Operational Excellence Is Carbon Management

Yield: Prevent scrap and rework.

Flow: Reduce waiting, idle loads and unnecessary movement.

Maintenance: Keep assets at designed efficiency.

Planning: Match batches, routes and capacity to demand.

Design: Remove carbon before it is locked into the process.

### Supplier Engagement Needs a Data Ladder

Screen: Use secondary factors to find material categories.

Request: Ask material suppliers for product or process data.

Validate: Check boundary, method, period and assurance.

Collaborate: Co-design lower-carbon specifications and logistics.

Contract: Embed evidence and performance expectations.

### Circular Design Changes the Carbon Equation

1. Refuse unnecessary input

2. Reduce material intensity

3. Reuse components

4. Repair and remanufacture

5. Recycle at highest practical value

### Industry 4.0 Can Help—and Create New Risks

| Opportunity | Implementation risk |
|---|---|
| IoT enables real-time energy and process data | High integration cost and interoperability gaps |
| Analytics detects anomalies and predicts failures | Data security and ownership concerns |
| ERP connects carbon to materials, orders and suppliers | Automation can scale poor-quality data |

### What an Enterprise Footprint Platform Connects

Transactions: Materials, purchase orders, production and logistics.

Factors: Versioned libraries matched to geography and period.

Flows: Energy and material movement across products and sites.

Results: Corporate and product footprints with drill-down.

Action: Hotspots integrated into operational decisions.

### Is the Technology Ready for This Site?

Does the measure meet technical, operational, financial and evidence thresholds?

If yes / fit: Pilot with success criteria, monitoring plan and scale-up gate.

If no / not yet: Redesign, sequence a dependency, or retain in the opportunity backlog.

A promising technology is not yet a feasible initiative until site constraints and implementation risk are tested.

### Design a Pilot That Can Be Judged

Baseline: Comparable pre-intervention period and operating conditions.

Intervention: Specific asset, control or behaviour change.

Metric: Energy, output, emissions, cost and quality guardrail.

Duration: Long enough to capture representative variation.

Decision rule: Scale, modify or stop threshold agreed in advance.

### A Portfolio Needs More Than One Score

Impact: Annual and cumulative tCO₂e.

Economics: NPV, payback and abatement cost.

Feasibility: Technology maturity, resources and disruption.

Strategic fit: Customer, compliance and resilience value.

Confidence: Data quality and dependency risk.

### A Transparent Multi-Criteria Decision Process

1. Agree criteria

2. Set weights

3. Define scoring anchors

4. Score with evidence

5. Test sensitivity and approve

### Make Trade-Offs Visible

Use a common scale and documented evidence.

Weighted score = Σ (criterion score × criterion weight)

- Weights must total 100%
- Use behavioural anchors for 1–5 scores
- Do not let the formula replace governance

### No-Regret Measures and Strategic Bets

| No-regret | Strategic bet |
|---|---|
| Strong economics and proven technology | Higher uncertainty or capital need |
| Low dependency and quick implementation | Enables deep reductions or future compliance |
| Build momentum and fund later work | Use staged gates and learning milestones |

### What Should Start First?

Is the measure high-impact, feasible now and independent of another project?

If yes / fit: Place in the immediate delivery wave with a named owner and budget gate.

If no / not yet: Place in enable, pilot, partner or monitor wave with a trigger date.

Sequence dependencies: data platform before optimisation, charging before fleet conversion, specification before supplier tender.

### Counter Common Decision Biases

Payback bias: Include lifecycle value and residual risk.

Pet-project bias: Require comparable evidence and scoring anchors.

Precision bias: Show ranges and confidence ratings.

Silo bias: Include operations, finance, procurement and users.

Offset bias: Apply the reduction hierarchy first.

### Prioritisation Is a Portfolio Conversation

Project: Model baseline, intervention and uncertainty.

Prove: Test technical and operating feasibility.

Compare: Use carbon, cash, risk, strategy and confidence.

Sequence: Balance no-regret delivery with deep-reduction enablers.

## Topic 3: Develop the Reduction Plan

EMS governance, action planning, Singapore requirements and review

Learning outcome: LO3

### An EMS Turns Intent Into Repeatable Control

Context: Interested parties, risks, opportunities and obligations.

Leadership: Policy, accountability and resources.

Planning: Aspects, objectives, targets and actions.

Support & operation: Competence, communication and operational controls.

Evaluation & improvement: Monitor, audit, review and correct.

### Carbon Management Through PDCA

1. Plan boundary, baseline, risks and targets

2. Do controls, projects and engagement

3. Check data, KPIs, audits and variance

4. Act on findings and reset priorities

### Ownership and Accountability Are Different

| Process owner | Carbon / GHG manager |
|---|---|
| Controls the activity and operational data | Maintains method, inventory and controls |
| Implements agreed actions | Coordinates reporting and assurance |
| Explains performance variance | Escalates quality and compliance issues |

### Build Controls Around the Data Journey

Capture: Authorised sources and complete populations.

Transform: Locked factors, units and calculation rules.

Review: Threshold checks and independent approval.

Change: Version control and recalculation log.

Retain: Evidence repository and retention period.

### The Plan Needs People Who Can Operate It

Executives: Approve ambition, capital and risk appetite.

Finance: Validate business cases and reporting controls.

Operations: Own sources, projects and root causes.

Procurement: Engage suppliers and embed specifications.

Data / IT: Maintain architecture, access and quality.

### A Useful Review Drives Decisions

1. Review target trajectory

2. Explain material variance

3. Assess project delivery

4. Consider risk and obligations

5. Decide resources and corrective action

### Audit the System, Not Only the Number

Boundary: Is it complete and consistently applied?

Evidence: Can samples be traced to source?

Calculation: Are factors, units and formulas controlled?

Action: Are corrective actions owned and closed?

Communication: Are claims supported and balanced?

### Is the Variance a Data Error or Performance Gap?

Does corrected evidence bring the KPI back within tolerance?

If yes / fit: Fix the data/control issue, assess restatement and prevent recurrence.

If no / not yet: Investigate operational root cause and implement a performance corrective action.

Do not hide a missed target by changing the data; do not launch a project to fix a calculation error.

### A Credible Reduction Plan Has Eight Parts

1 · Boundary and baseline: What is covered and measured.

2 · Hotspots: Where emissions concentrate and why.

3 · Target: Outcome, year, scope and trajectory.

4 · Initiatives: Measures, savings and dependencies.

5 · Roadmap: Sequence, milestones and resources.

6 · Governance: Owners and decision rights.

7 · Measurement: KPIs, evidence and review.

8 · Risk & communication: Uncertainty, claims and escalation.

### A Target Needs Scope and Substance

| Weak target | Decision-ready target |
|---|---|
| Reduce emissions significantly | 35% absolute Scope 1+2 reduction by 2030 from 2025 |
| No base year or boundary | Boundary and recalculation policy stated |
| No trajectory or owner | Annual trajectory and accountable executive |

### Sequence the Reduction Plan

1. Stabilise data and controls

2. Deliver no-regret efficiency

3. Pilot strategic technology

4. Transform supply and product choices

5. Address residual emissions

### Connect Outcome, Driver and Delivery Metrics

Outcome: Total tCO₂e and tCO₂e per functional unit.

Driver: kWh/unit, fuel/km, kg material/unit, waste yield.

Delivery: Project milestone, installed capacity, supplier coverage.

Quality: Primary-data share, estimate share and control exceptions.

### Make Failure Modes Visible Before Launch

Delivery: Delay, skills, vendor or permit.

Performance: Savings lower than modelled.

Commercial: Tariff, price, demand or finance.

Data: Missing evidence or incompatible systems.

Claims: Overstatement, double counting or unclear offsets.

### Reduction and Offset Are Not Interchangeable

| Operational reduction | Offset / credit |
|---|---|
| Changes emissions within the inventory or value chain | Finances a reduction or removal outside the inventory |
| Traceable to a measure and baseline | Requires quality and retirement evidence |
| Supports the reduction trajectory | Disclose separately from gross emissions |

### A Responsible Claim Shows the Full Picture

Boundary: Which entities, scopes and period?

Metric: Gross, net, absolute or intensity?

Method: Which standard, factors and recalculations?

Progress: Actual result versus trajectory.

Limitations: Estimates, uncertainty and exclusions.

### Translate a 35% Target Into Annual Milestones

Illustrative 2025 baseline: 10,000 tCO₂e. 2030 target: 6,500 tCO₂e.

Required reduction = 3,500 tCO₂e
Linear planning guide = 700 tCO₂e/year average

- Actual roadmap need not be linear
- Link each step change to initiatives
- Track business-as-usual growth separately

### Budget the Enablers, Not Only the Hardware

Measurement: Meters, factors, assurance and data engineering.

People: Project management, training and supplier engagement.

Change: Communications, workflow redesign and incentives.

Contingency: Performance shortfall and dependency risk.

Maintenance: Commissioning and sustained optimisation.

### Every Action Needs a Complete Contract

What: Specific intervention and boundary.

Why: Hotspot and expected outcome.

Who: Accountable owner and support roles.

When: Start, milestone, completion and review.

How much: Budget, resources and quantified benefit.

Evidence: KPI, source and verification method.

### Make Objectives SMARTER

A target should guide action and adaptation.

Specific · Measurable · Achievable · Relevant · Time-bound · Evaluated · Reviewed

- Use an outcome metric and at least one driver metric
- Name the evidence source and review cadence
- Predefine escalation and revision conditions

### Clarify Roles Before Delivery Starts

1. Accountable owner approves

2. Responsible team delivers

3. Consulted experts shape decisions

4. Informed stakeholders receive updates

### Use a Single Initiative Register

Identity: Initiative ID, source, site and owner.

Value: Capex, savings, tCO₂e and confidence.

Delivery: Stage, milestone, dependency and issue.

Decision: Approval, gate and rationale.

Evidence: Baseline, M&V method and realised result.

### Should the Initiative Scale?

Did the pilot meet carbon, cost, operational and data-quality criteria?

If yes / fit: Approve rollout with updated benefit case and implementation controls.

If no / not yet: Modify or stop; document learning and update the opportunity register.

Stage gates protect capital while preserving learning from uncertainty.

### Measure Realised Savings Fairly

Baseline model: Representative pre-project performance.

Adjustment: Production, weather, occupancy or route mix.

Boundary: Avoid leakage and double counting.

Persistence: Check that savings continue after launch.

Approval: Independent review of material results.

### Know the Current Singapore Thresholds

Reportable facility: Annual direct emissions at or above 2,000 tCO₂e.

Taxable facility: Annual direct emissions at or above 25,000 tCO₂e.

2026–2027 tax rate: S$45 per tCO₂e.

Toward 2030: Government has indicated S$50–S$80 per tCO₂e by 2030.

Scope: Industrial facilities in specified sectors; verify current CPA requirements.

### Reportable and Taxable Facilities

| ≥2,000 tCO₂e direct | ≥25,000 tCO₂e direct |
|---|---|
| Register as reportable where applicable | Also subject to carbon tax |
| Submit annual Emissions Report by 30 June following year | Monitoring Plan and verified Emissions Report |
| Appoint required representatives and manager | Stronger controls and verification requirements |

### Screen the 2026 Carbon-Tax Exposure

Illustrative taxable emissions: 30,000 tCO₂e. 2026 rate: S$45/tCO₂e.

Gross screened liability = 30,000 × S$45 = S$1,350,000

- Use verified reportable emissions and current law for filing
- Account for eligible allowances only with competent advice
- Use the result to stress-test investments—not as the inventory boundary

Govern: Owners, controls, competence and management review.

Plan: Boundary, target, initiatives, roadmap and resources.

Deliver: Action contracts, stage gates and dependencies.

Learn: Measurement, audit, corrective action and transparent claims.

### From Target to Continual Improvement

1. Approve target

2. Fund roadmap

3. Deliver actions

4. Verify outcomes

5. Review and adapt
